# White Center Food Bank — Leadership Discussion Package

**Civic Continuum & Maya Node**  
**Exploratory Food-Bank Workflow Evaluation**  
Prepared October 30, 2026  
**Review copy:** for founder review and approval before any external email or presentation.

## 1. Purpose

Invite White Center Food Bank (WCFB) leadership to become an early thought partner in exploring a people-first approach to clearer handoffs and accountable follow-through. The larger ambition is to learn whether a practical, adaptable pattern could eventually help community-serving organizations coordinate important work with greater clarity. The first step is intentionally modest: a no-cost conversation and fictional demonstration of one staff-selected workflow. This is an invitation to help shape an idea—not a claim that a proven platform or world-changing result already exists.

## 2. Respect for current operations

WCFB coordinates food distribution, volunteer participation, home-delivery activity, and community partnerships. Its staff, policies, established systems, and partner relationships remain authoritative. This proposal does not presume deficiencies in CERVIS or any internal process. WCFB staff understand their operations best; any evaluation begins by listening and letting them define whether a problem exists and what would count as useful.

## 3. The concept

Maya Node explores a human-centered accountability pattern:

**REQUEST → AUTHORITY → PERMISSION → EVIDENCE → HUMAN REVIEW → OUTCOME → CORRECTION**

The intent is to make ownership, decision authority, handoffs, and next steps easier to understand while leaving policy decisions and operational authority with people. Technical capability does not confer authority.

## 4. What exists now — and what does not

### Available for discussion
- A browser-based offline workflow lab using fictional data and simulated interactions.
- A prototype foundation for demonstrating limited permission and review concepts in a local/test environment.
- A repeatable discussion structure for examining one fictional workflow.

### Not available or claimed
- No live connection to WCFB systems, CERVIS, databases, or partner platforms.
- No live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, delivery confirmation, food-safety monitoring, inspection, acceptance approval, or compliance certification.
- No claim of immutable production receipts, permanent audit retention, regulatory certification, or production hosting readiness.
- No claim that WCFB has undocumented overrides, paper-only processes, or specific operational gaps. These questions belong to staff discovery, not assumptions.

## 5. Three discussion scenarios

### Scenario A — Volunteer handoff
**Question:** Would a clearer record of who prepared, approved, and accepted a handoff help staff resolve questions?

A fictional batch and volunteer identifier can demonstrate a request, human approval, handoff acknowledgement, and review. This is not live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, proof of delivery, or a production custody record.

### Scenario B — Grocery-rescue exception
**Question:** When an unusual delivery or quantity discrepancy occurs, what information helps the authorized person decide what happens next?

A fictional exception can be routed for a human decision and described in a structured record. The prototype does not monitor temperatures, inspect food, determine food safety, authorize acceptance, or certify compliance. Food-safety policy and acceptance decisions remain with WCFB's authorized people.

### Scenario C — Follow-through on a concern
**Question:** How does a concern move from intake to an assigned owner, an authorized response, and a documented closeout?

A fictional concern can illustrate assignment, review, a stated corrective step, and a human-entered completion note. This is not a real complaint-management service, staff-notification system, or repair-verification tool.

## 6. Proposed evaluation boundaries

- No-cost initial conversation and agreed fictional demonstration.
- Synthetic/fictitious data only; no client, donor, volunteer, staff, address, or other personal information.
- No access to internal systems, accounts, networks, databases, or records.
- No installation, live integration, outbound email, automated messages, or changes to existing procedures.
- No obligation to continue; WCFB can decline or stop at any point.
- Any later activity involving real data, systems, staff operations, or service delivery requires a separate written scope and appropriate reviews.

## 7. Proposed success measures

- Staff can explain the demonstrated workflow and identify the human decision-maker.
- The demonstration makes the handoff, assigned owner, and next step easier to identify.
- The activity requires no real personal data or access to WCFB systems.
- Staff can identify at least one possible benefit and one burden or limitation.
- WCFB can freely decline or stop without cost, obligation, or operational change.

## 8. Suggested next step

Ask WCFB leadership whether one of the three areas is worth discussing. If yes, let staff select the workflow and agree in writing on purpose, participants, duration, boundaries, success measures, and stop conditions before any demonstration.
