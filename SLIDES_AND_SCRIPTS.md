# Eight-Slide Leadership Presentation and Presenter Script

## Slide 1 — Help Shape What Comes Next

**On-slide content**
- Civic Continuum & Maya Node
- An opportunity to help shape a practical, human-centered approach that could inform community-serving organizations beyond one pilot
- Prepared for White Center Food Bank leadership

**Presenter script**  
Thank you for taking the time to meet. I'm Mya Brown, founder of Civic Continuum. I'm developing Maya Node around a straightforward principle: technical capability does not confer authority. I believe the next generation of community-serving tools should be shaped with the people who understand the work. Today is an invitation to help challenge and shape that idea. We start with one small, clearly bounded workflow discussion and fictional demonstration—not a system replacement or a request to change your procedures.

## Slide 2 — Respecting the Work Already in Place

**On-slide content**
- WCFB coordinates food distribution, volunteer participation, home-delivery activity, and community partnerships.
- Existing systems, staff judgment, policies, and partner relationships remain authoritative.
- Listen first; make no assumptions about internal deficiencies.
- The organization defines the problem; the prototype is evaluated against that reality.

**Presenter script**  
White Center Food Bank coordinates a substantial community operation, including food distribution, volunteers, home-delivery activity, and community partnerships. I do not want to presume that your systems are deficient or that I understand your internal processes better than the people who run them. The first step is to listen and let staff identify whether there is a workflow worth examining.

## Slide 3 — A Small Layer of Clarity, Not a System Replacement

**On-slide content**
- What stays: existing staff judgment and policies, CERVIS and current procedures, partner relationships, organizational decision authority.
- What we are exploring: whether a structured record of a request, authorized decision, handoff, review, and outcome could help in one staff-selected workflow.
- Current boundary: no live integrations, dispatch, routing, delivery verification, food-safety approval, or production audit service.

**Presenter script**  
The idea is not to replace CERVIS, your policies, staff judgment, or partner processes. We are exploring whether a structured way to describe a request, the person authorized to decide, the handoff, and the follow-through could be useful in a narrow situation. The current prototype is not a live food-bank system and does not provide live integrations or operational dispatch.

## Slide 4 — Human Authority Built Into the Workflow Concept

**On-slide content**
“Technical capability does not confer authority.”

REQUEST → AUTHORITY → PERMISSION → EVIDENCE → HUMAN REVIEW → OUTCOME → CORRECTION

- The question: can this sequence make ownership, decision authority, and next steps easier to understand?
- Boundary: a design concept and limited prototype demonstration—not proof of live authorization enforcement, permanent records, or production readiness.

**Presenter script**  
The sequence we want to explore is: request, authority, permission, evidence, human review, outcome, and correction. The point is not to automate judgment. It is to make responsibility and next steps easier to understand. This slide describes a concept and a limited prototype demonstration, not a claim of production enforcement or permanent audit records.

## Slide 5 — Scenario 1: Volunteer Handoff

**On-slide content**
- Discussion question: would a clearer record of who prepared, approved, and accepted a handoff help staff resolve questions?
- Fictional walkthrough: batch prepared → authorized human approval → handoff acknowledgement → review.
- Not included: live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, proof of delivery, or production custody record.
- Evaluation question: would this help, or simply add work?

**Presenter script**  
One possible discussion scenario is a volunteer handoff. Using fictional identifiers only, we can demonstrate how a prepared batch might be described, how an authorized person might approve a handoff, and how the receiving person might acknowledge it. This is not live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, or proof of delivery. We would want staff to tell us whether this kind of record would solve a real problem or simply add work.

## Slide 6 — Scenario 2: Grocery-Rescue Exception

**On-slide content**
- Discussion question: when an unusual delivery or quantity discrepancy occurs, what information helps the authorized person decide what happens next?
- Fictional walkthrough: exception described → referred to authorized human decision-maker → decision and next step recorded.
- Not included: temperature monitoring, inspection, food-safety determination, acceptance authorization, or compliance certification.
- Food-safety policy and acceptance decisions remain with authorized WCFB personnel.

**Presenter script**  
A second fictional scenario concerns an unusual delivery or quantity discrepancy. The demonstration can show how an exception might be brought to an authorized human decision-maker and how the next step might be documented. It does not monitor temperatures, inspect food, determine whether food is safe, authorize acceptance, or certify compliance. Those decisions remain with your staff and established policies.

## Slide 7 — Scenario 3: Follow-Through on a Concern

**On-slide content**
- Discussion question: how does a concern move from intake to an assigned owner, an authorized response, and a documented closeout?
- Fictional walkthrough: concern received → owner assigned → human review → corrective step stated → human-entered completion note.
- Not included: real complaint management, staff notification, repair verification, or guarantee that any concern is automatically resolved.

**Presenter script**  
A third scenario looks at follow-through. A fictional concern is received, assigned to an owner, reviewed, given an authorized next step, and closed with a human-entered completion note. This does not manage real complaints, notify staff, verify repairs, or guarantee that nothing is missed. It simply lets us discuss whether a clearer record of ownership and completion would help.

## Slide 8 — Be an Early Voice in Shaping What Comes Next

**On-slide content**
- Start with one conversation.
- Let WCFB staff select the workflow.
- No-cost initial discussion and agreed fictional demonstration.
- Synthetic data only; no system access or installation; no outbound organizational messages; no obligation to continue.

**Presenter script**  
If one of these areas is worth exploring, I would be excited to learn alongside your team. Your experience can help us distinguish a genuinely useful idea from one that merely sounds impressive. Any future growth must be earned through listening, testing, and evidence. The initial discussion and agreed demonstration would be no-cost, use synthetic data only, require no access to your systems, and create no obligation to continue. If it is not useful, your existing processes remain exactly as they are. Thank you for considering it.
