# Three Fictional Food-Pantry Workflow Scenarios

These are discussion exercises only. All names, IDs, batches, quantities, dates, and events used in any demonstration must be synthetic. These examples do not describe confirmed incidents or deficiencies at White Center Food Bank.

## Scenario 1 — Volunteer handoff

### Fictional situation
A prepared food order is ready for a volunteer handoff. The team wants to be able to reconstruct, during a later question, who prepared the batch, who had authority to approve its release, who acknowledged receiving it, and what the next step was.

### Example workflow
1. **Request:** Create a fictional handoff record for batch DEMO-104.
2. **Preparation:** Record a synthetic preparer ID and a brief description of the prepared batch.
3. **Authority:** Identify the role/person who is authorized under the organization's own policy to approve the handoff.
4. **Permission:** Show that the system concept records the approval step; it does not create authority or override policy.
5. **Evidence:** Add a simulated acknowledgement by a fictional receiving volunteer.
6. **Human review:** A supervisor reviews the example record.
7. **Outcome/correction:** Record the human-entered outcome or correction if information is missing.

### Potential organizational value to test
- Clearer visibility into handoff ownership.
- Easier reconstruction of what was acknowledged and what remains unresolved.
- A shared view of the next step without replacing existing procedures.

### Questions for staff
- Where is handoff information already recorded?
- What questions are hardest to answer after a handoff?
- Would another record reduce confusion or create duplicate work?
- Who is authorized to approve each step?

### Boundary
This is not live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, proof of delivery, or a production custody record.

## Scenario 2 — Grocery-rescue exception

### Fictional situation
A fictional incoming donation or grocery-rescue delivery has a quantity discrepancy or another exception that requires a human decision. The example is not a report of an actual WCFB problem.

### Example workflow
1. **Request/intake:** Create a fictional exception ticket, e.g. EXC-DEMO-22.
2. **Evidence:** Record only synthetic details about the discrepancy, such as expected versus observed case count.
3. **Authority:** Route the question to the role that WCFB policy designates to decide.
4. **Human review:** The authorized person determines what action, if any, is appropriate.
5. **Outcome:** Record the decision and the next step as entered by that person.
6. **Correction:** If the record is incomplete, show who is responsible for clarifying it.

### Potential organizational value to test
- Makes the exception owner and decision point easier to identify.
- Keeps the reason for a human decision and the next step together.
- Helps distinguish an unresolved question from a reviewed decision.

### Questions for staff
- What exceptions are already tracked, and in which system?
- Which roles can make the relevant decisions?
- What information is actually useful to the decision-maker?
- Would a structured exception record fit existing work or duplicate it?

### Boundary
The prototype does not monitor temperatures, inspect food, determine food safety, authorize acceptance, or certify compliance. Food-safety policy and acceptance decisions remain with authorized personnel.

## Scenario 3 — Follow-through on a concern

### Fictional situation
A fictional concern is raised about a process, facility issue, service question, or follow-up item. The exercise explores how a concern might be assigned, reviewed, acted on, and closed.

### Example workflow
1. **Intake:** Create a synthetic concern, e.g. FOLLOWUP-DEMO-07.
2. **Ownership:** Assign a fictional owner or role for follow-up.
3. **Authority:** Identify who can approve the proposed response or corrective action.
4. **Human review:** Record the review and any decision in plain language.
5. **Correction:** State the corrective step and the person responsible.
6. **Closeout:** Add a human-entered completion note and mark whether further review remains.

### Potential organizational value to test
- Makes the responsible owner and next action visible.
- Helps distinguish assigned, in-progress, awaiting-decision, and closed items.
- Provides a concise record of the human-entered reason for closeout.
- May support learning from recurring themes if WCFB chooses to use such a process.

### Questions for staff
- How are concerns currently received and assigned?
- What makes an item genuinely complete?
- Who verifies completion, if anyone, under current policy?
- Would a summary of open owners and next steps be useful?

### Boundary
This is not a real complaint-management service, staff-notification system, repair-verification tool, or guarantee that concerns are automatically resolved.

---

## Shared evaluation test

For each scenario, ask staff to rate:
- Is the workflow relevant to your actual work?
- Is the proposed record clearer than what you already use?
- Does it reduce handoff ambiguity?
- Does it create duplicate entry or extra burden?
- Is the human decision-maker obvious?
- What must never be automated?
- What would make this unsuitable for your organization?

A successful exploratory discussion may conclude that the prototype adds no value. That is a valid result.
