Slide 3 · Fit

A Small Layer of Clarity, Not a System Replacement

  • What stays: existing staff judgment and policies, CERVIS and current procedures, partner relationships, organizational decision authority.
  • What we are exploring: whether a structured record of a request, authorized decision, handoff, review, and outcome could help in one staff-selected workflow.
  • Current boundary: no live integrations, dispatch, routing, delivery verification, food-safety approval, or production audit service.
Presenter scriptThe idea is not to replace CERVIS, your policies, staff judgment, or partner processes. We are exploring whether a structured way to describe a request, the person authorized to decide, the handoff, and the follow-through could be useful in a narrow situation. The current prototype is not a live food-bank system and does not provide live integrations or operational dispatch.
Slide 4 · Principle

Human Authority Built Into the Workflow Concept

“Technical capability does not confer authority.”

REQUEST → AUTHORITY → PERMISSION → EVIDENCE → HUMAN REVIEW → OUTCOME → CORRECTION

  • Can this sequence make ownership, decision authority, and next steps easier to understand?
  • This is a design concept and limited prototype demonstration—not proof of live authorization enforcement, permanent records, or production readiness.
Presenter scriptThe sequence we want to explore is: request, authority, permission, evidence, human review, outcome, and correction. The point is not to automate judgment. It is to make responsibility and next steps easier to understand. This slide describes a concept and a limited prototype demonstration, not a claim of production enforcement or permanent audit records.
Slide 5 · Scenario 1

Volunteer Handoff

  • Question: would a clearer record of who prepared, approved, and accepted a handoff help staff resolve questions?
  • Fictional walkthrough: batch prepared → authorized human approval → handoff acknowledgement → review.
  • Not included: live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, proof of delivery, or production custody record.
  • Would this help, or simply add work?
Presenter scriptOne possible discussion scenario is a volunteer handoff. Using fictional identifiers only, we can demonstrate how a prepared batch might be described, how an authorized person might approve a handoff, and how the receiving person might acknowledge it. This is not live dispatch, route optimization, driver tracking, or proof of delivery. We would want staff to tell us whether this kind of record would solve a real problem or simply add work.
Slide 6 · Scenario 2

Grocery-Rescue Exception

  • Question: when an unusual delivery or quantity discrepancy occurs, what information helps the authorized person decide what happens next?
  • Fictional walkthrough: exception described → referred to authorized human decision-maker → decision and next step recorded.
  • Not included: temperature monitoring, inspection, food-safety determination, acceptance authorization, or compliance certification.
  • Food-safety policy and acceptance decisions remain with authorized WCFB personnel.
Presenter scriptA second fictional scenario concerns an unusual delivery or quantity discrepancy. The demonstration can show how an exception might be brought to an authorized human decision-maker and how the next step might be documented. It does not monitor temperatures, inspect food, determine whether food is safe, authorize acceptance, or certify compliance. Those decisions remain with your staff and established policies.
Slide 7 · Scenario 3

Follow-Through on a Concern

  • Question: how does a concern move from intake to an assigned owner, an authorized response, and a documented closeout?
  • Fictional walkthrough: concern received → owner assigned → human review → corrective step stated → human-entered completion note.
  • Not included: real complaint management, staff notification, repair verification, or guarantee that any concern is automatically resolved.
Presenter scriptA third scenario looks at follow-through. A fictional concern is received, assigned to an owner, reviewed, given an authorized next step, and closed with a human-entered completion note. This does not manage real complaints, notify staff, verify repairs, or guarantee that nothing is missed. It simply lets us discuss whether a clearer record of ownership and completion would help.
